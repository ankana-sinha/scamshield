The problem

Scams don't target ignorance. They target the moment.

Elderly citizens are disproportionately targeted. Scammers use urgency, authority, and fear — a fake bank call, a threatening SMS, a convincing WhatsApp forward — to pressure people into acting before they can think.

The problem isn't that people don't know about scams. It's that scams are designed to bypass careful thinking entirely.

Your bank calls. Your KYC is "about to expire." They ask you to share an OTP. You're not sure if it's real. But in those few seconds, you feel pressured to act.

How ScamShield reads a suspicious message

Here's how ScamShield would break down a common scam attempt:

ScamShield Analysis
Suspicious message received

"Dear Customer, your SBI account KYC has expired. Your account will be blocked within 24 hours. Click here to update immediately: http://sbi-kyc-update.in/verify. Reply with OTP sent to your mobile."

ScamShield analysis
  • Banks never ask for OTPs over SMS or links
  • Urgency tactic: "blocked within 24 hours" creates pressure
  • Suspicious URL — does not match SBI's official domain
  • Asks you to click a link and share sensitive information
Safe next step: Do not click the link or share any OTP. Call your bank directly using the number on your debit card or passbook. Report at cybercrime.gov.in
